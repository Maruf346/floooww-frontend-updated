# Dashboard UI Guidelines

- Individual creator Invite actions always navigate to the shared three-step invite route. The route is data-driven by project and creator, preserves the BrandShell gutter, and implements setup/editable deliverables, review-and-pay, then invite-sent confirmation. Setup fields use the shared Dropdown and MultiDropdown controls for deliverable type, platform, usage, products, coupon codes, shipments, and payment method; incomplete deliverables keep review disabled.
- The Invite flow stepper is a full-width, centered three-stage progress bar: `20px` numbered or completed circles and `12px` labels on desktop, `2px` connectors that expand between steps, blue completed/current states, and muted upcoming state. Scale down modestly on narrow screens; do not use large grid step cards or leave trailing space after Done.
- The individual Invite setup has both an empty and a populated long-term state. Filled rows remain editable and use the same deliverables table; a recurring cycle adds a light-blue outlined schedule block with three cycle cards, an amber escrow warning, and footer totals expressed as deliverables per cycle, cycle fee, and total creator fees.
- The Invite deliverable-type selector uses the supplied grouped platform menu (Instagram Reel/Story/Carousel/Static post; YouTube Short/Video; Pinterest Idea Pin; Snapchat Story; Live; custom). Render that menu in a body portal at a top-level z-index so it is never clipped by the responsive deliverables table or covered by dashboard layers.
- The adjacent Invite platform selector is also a top-level portal. Show the connected account with its follower count and a selected checkmark; retain unavailable platforms as muted, disabled rows with a `Not connected for [creator]` explanation.
- Invite deadlines use a portalled, Monday-first calendar picker with month navigation, selected-date styling, out-of-month dates muted, and a cycle-aware note. Deadline choices update the deliverable immediately; dates beyond the project end are blocked.
- Shared `Dropdown` and `MultiDropdown` menus render through body portals at the top dashboard layer, tracking their trigger while scrolling or resizing. Use this behavior for every Invite form dropdown. The Invite usage-rights control uses `Organic`, `Paid Ads`, `Paid ads`, `Perpetual`, plus the pencil-icon custom option.
- The Invite shipment field uses a dedicated top-layer multi-select rather than the generic multi-select: show a `Shipping: N shipments` trigger summary, selected shipment rows with courier/tracking metadata, checkboxes, an Add shipment action, and the automatic tracking helper copy.
- The Invite product field uses a dedicated, top-layer Shopify catalogue selector rather than the generic multi-select: it has a catalogue search, `Products:` trigger summary, selected stock rows with URL/price metadata and checkboxes, an Add product-link action, and helper copy explaining product-specific tracking links and shared coupons.
- The Invite coupon field uses a dedicated top-layer Shopify code selector rather than the generic multi-select: it has a `Coupons:` trigger summary, selected code rows with discount/product/usage metadata and checkboxes, a Generate-code action, and helper copy explaining independent sales tracking.
- The Invite recurring-deliverables setting is a left-to-right switch, never a ticked checkbox or radio. Keep whole-card toggling and keyboard-accessible switch behavior; enabling it only repeats deliverables the user has entered and must never auto-populate form rows.
- Invite review-and-pay is calculated from completed deliverables: one-time shows `N deliverables · earliest–latest deadline · creator fee`, GST, and a single `Charged now` total; recurring shows `Cycle 1`, its deliverable count, Floooww fee, GST, and `Charged now · cycle 1`, with later-cycle totals only in the recurring state.
- Invite step two is a payment review rather than a generic confirmation: use a fee/GST ledger, highlighted recurring-charge agreement, selectable payment methods, and a footer that calculates today’s charge plus the three-cycle total. Disable payment until the agreement is acknowledged.
- Invite step three is a paid-and-invited confirmation: replace the stepper with the sent-state title, a green escrow confirmation, a compact “What happens next” panel, and a footer linking to invited creators. Recurring invites include three calculated cycle-charge cards and future-charge copy; one-time invites omit cycle cards entirely and show only the calculated paid/escrow amount, acceptance refund, and deliverable-deadline approval flow.
- Invited-creator deliverable edits use a dedicated full-width BrandShell route with chronological cycle cards. Completed/approved items are visibly locked; editable and newly added future items preserve the shared deliverable controls, gain a blue unsaved-change outline while being edited, and culminate in the persistent repeating-cycle footer with Stop repeating, Cancel, and Send changes actions.

- Creator profile UIs are data-driven. The applicant drawer and full-profile route must resolve the selected creator from shared profile data rather than reuse a single creator's copy, metrics, channels, services, or rail information. Connected channels stay selectable; absent channels render disabled with `Not connected`.

- Dashboard page content uses the BrandShell's shared responsive outer gutter (`p-5 sm:p-8`) and must remain `w-full` rather than adding an independent page-level maximum width. Grid columns may be constrained for their design, but the page itself must line up with the left and right edges used by other dashboard pages.
- Full creator profiles use the BrandShell and a responsive `minmax(0, 1fr) / 340px` desktop main-and-rail layout. The application summary, intro video, four profile tabs, profile cards, creator rail, and floating assistant follow the supplied BR-05c composition. The rail's channel list is interactive: All channels is the default and single-channel selections update channel-specific followers, rates, services, fees, social metrics, and portfolio content; Reviews and project fit remain unchanged.

- Applicant drawer Reviews tab: use the supplied 4.8 score, orange five-row distribution (19/3/1/0/0), muted quality tiles, and three review cards. Animate bars and cards once while preserving the fixed footer actions.

- Animate dashboard content when it enters the viewport. Use slow, restrained motion: approximately 0.65–0.9 seconds for card reveals and 1.2–1.4 seconds for graph strokes.
- Prefer opacity plus a small upward translation for cards. Stagger related tiles by about 60–90ms.
- Animate data visualizations progressively where practical: line paths draw, bars grow from zero, and donut/chart content gently scales in. Respect `viewport: { once: true }` to avoid distracting repeated motion while scrolling.
- Apply this visualization-level motion consistently to supporting analytics cards as well: ranked bars, stacked segments, demographic columns, funnels, comparison bars, and budget donuts should animate their data while their shell remains visually stable.
- On Brand project overview desktop layouts, keep the right-rail “What to do next” card sticky with a `24px` top offset while the main project content scrolls. Leave it in normal document flow below the desktop breakpoint.
- The empty Brand Project Applications tab uses a compact Figma-style status rail and filter toolbar above a centered `294px` minimum-height empty-state card. Keep the title at `14px`, description at `13px`, `56px` muted icon tile, 40px actions, and `12px` counts; all filters are functional custom drawers.
- The populated Brand Project Applications tab uses the supplied 1116px composition: a `40px` toolbar, then a three-column grid of `461px` applicant cards with 16px gutters. Each card keeps its fixed Figma vertical rhythm for the 56px applicant header/match ring, platform line, source caption, 56px stat strip, note, 78px fee panel, and two 32px action rows. Stack at smaller widths without shrinking the card typography.
- Brand Project Applications is context-driven without changing its layout: UGC/AI Video swaps Followers and Eng. rate filters for Portfolio and Turnaround, Gift swaps Fee for Asks, channel/stats/fee copy responds to Type/Partnership/Duration, and the footer says `applications close` for one-time or `open until` for long-term. Action order is Message, View profile, then Decline / Shortlist / Invite; use semantic SVG icons and blue/red/neutral button treatments rather than text-glyph replacements.
- Application tab badges, status-rail totals, and the `Showing … of …` footer must derive from the project’s application count; never hard-code a design-reference count.
- Project application action rows match the reference by status: New and Reviewed cards use three equal neutral-outline Decline and Shortlist buttons plus a blue Invite button. Shortlisted cards use a borderless Remove text action beside the blue Invite button. Declined cards use neutral-outline Undo decline beside a blue Shortlist button. Both rows are `32px` tall with `8px` gaps.
- Project application action buttons are text-only when the supplied reference is text-only; do not add Message, profile, or decision icons unless the Figma/MCP output explicitly includes them.
- When a Figma/MCP output supplies an icon for a control, preserve that semantic icon in the implementation; do not substitute a generic icon solely because the label still makes sense.
- Use Lucide or Tabler SVG icons for UI controls such as dropdowns, sort affordances, actions, and navigation. Never use Unicode/text glyphs as icon substitutes in production dashboard UI.
- The Brand Project Applications “View profile” control opens the reusable applicant slide-over: `640px` maximum desktop width, full viewport width on small screens, a dimmed backdrop, a slow right-to-left entrance, and a body-scroll lock. Preserve the Figma’s `24px` desktop gutters, `64px` identity avatar, `72px` match ring, status/verified badges, fee and intro-video blocks, and horizontal profile tabs. All drawer content, including its header and tabs, scrolls together; only the fixed three-action footer remains sticky. Animate the fit bars from zero once the drawer opens.
- The applicant drawer’s Social stats tab follows the supplied `592px` inner width: a `60px` four-platform selector, platform summary and reach/activity metric cards, a `292px` country-map card, `192px` age/city pair, `110px` gender card, and `234px` follower-growth card. Keep the white outlined cards, muted metric surfaces, indigo data palette, and slow one-time bar/column/map reveals.

- The applicant drawer’s Portfolio tab uses a `60px` connected-platform strip followed by a 3×2 Recent posts gallery at the `592px` inner desktop width. Each post has a rounded portrait tile, a `14px` view count, and `12px` caption; show the supplied six captions and values, and animate tiles in with a restrained stagger. At smaller widths, switch the gallery to two columns without shrinking its text.
- When YouTube is selected in the applicant Portfolio tab, use the active YouTube platform segment and replace the Reels grid with a two-tile `Videos` section (`Long-form uploads`) followed by a three-tile `Shorts` section (`Vertical videos under 3 minutes`). The supplied values are `38K views — Festive GRWM · 12 min`, `12K views — Serum review · 8 min`, `22K views — Night routine`, `19K views — Glow in 7 days`, and `9K views — Mist hacks`.
